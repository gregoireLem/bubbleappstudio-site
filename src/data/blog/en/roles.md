**Villagers, Werewolves and the Seer form a simple starting set of Werewolf roles.** Other familiar characters include the Witch, Hunter, Cupid and Little Girl. A role tells you your objective and any special action; it does not necessarily tell you who everyone else is.

The roles below appear on the Werewolf Undercover site. This guide describes common Werewolf conventions, not a complete specification of every app setting. Abilities, timing, and win conditions vary by version; always read the role instructions in your game.

## Seven common Werewolf roles at a glance

- **Villager:** uses discussion and the daytime vote; normally has no night action.
- **Werewolf:** works with the wolf team to choose a night victim and hide during discussion.
- **Seer:** checks a player at night; the rules determine whether the result reveals a role or an alignment.
- **Witch:** has limited powers to save or eliminate a player; check uses and timing.
- **Hunter:** can take another player with them when eliminated, according to the edition's rules.
- **Cupid:** links two lovers; their shared fate can also affect their winning objective.
- **Little Girl:** may secretly observe the wolves during an authorised phase, with a risk of discovery.

These are seven common characters, not a universal list for every Werewolf game. [Andrew Plotkin's rules](https://eblong.com/zarf/werewolf.html) use the Villager, Werewolf and Seer as the basic set; the [Le Pacte rulebook, in French](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-asmodeeca/uploads/2023/07/WerewolvesThePact_FR_Rules.pdf) describes the additional characters and their particular rules. Read on for how to play each role.

Before the first night, check three things privately: **Whose side am I on? When do I act? What information am I allowed to receive?** For example, a Seer who learns that someone is not a wolf has not necessarily learned their exact character. Keeping those two claims separate makes the next discussion clearer.

## Villager: turn conversation into evidence

A regular Villager usually has no night action. Your tools are listening, questions, and the daytime vote. Keep track of who accused whom and whether their explanations changed. Ask someone to explain a specific vote rather than demanding that they prove their innocence.

Do not mistake a lack of special powers for a lack of influence. Villagers connect the clues and decide which stories hold together.

For turning a suspicion into a useful question, read our [Villager strategy guide](/en/blog/werewolf-villager-role-strategy/), with a worked example of an accusation changing targets before the vote.

## Werewolf: keep your story consistent

Werewolves typically know their teammates and choose a victim at night. During the day, they try to blend into the village. A useful beginner approach is to participate naturally and remember your earlier reasoning.

Blindly defending another wolf can link you together. So can switching an accusation without explanation. Bluffing works best when your contributions sound like the way you normally talk.

For choosing a night target and explaining your votes consistently, read our [strategy guide for playing as a wolf](/en/blog/werewolf-wolf-strategy/), with an example of responding to accusations against a teammate.

If your game includes the Guard, a wolf attack can run into protection. Our [guide to playing the Guard](/en/blog/werewolf-guard-role/) looks at the same choice from the other side of the table.

## Seer: information with a risk

In many versions, the Seer checks a player at night to learn information about their identity or alignment. The exact information depends on the rules. Use what you learn to guide your questions, but consider the risk of becoming a target if you reveal your role.

Knowing one player’s alignment does not solve the whole game. Watch how others react to that player and distinguish confirmed information from your own guesses.

For choosing a useful check and deciding when to share your findings, see our [Seer strategy guide](/en/blog/werewolf-seer-role-strategy/), including an example across two nights.

## Witch and Hunter: know the timing

The Witch commonly has limited-use powers to save a player or eliminate one. Check how many uses you have, whether self-saving is allowed, and when you must decide. Those details can change the outcome of a night.

To weigh healing against poison, read our [Witch potion guide](/en/blog/werewolf-witch-role-potions/), with rule differences and a worked example of choosing the wrong target.

The Hunter commonly eliminates another player when they die, subject to the version’s rules. Think about your strongest evidence before that moment arrives. A dramatic accusation is not necessarily a reliable clue.

Our [Hunter role guide](/en/blog/werewolf-hunter-role/) explains when the ability activates, how to prepare a target, and how the wrong shot changes the balance of the game.

## Cupid and Little Girl: introduce them later

Cupid commonly links two lovers whose fates are connected. Depending on their teams, they may have a different winning objective. Explain what happens when one lover dies before you begin.

For choosing the pair and understanding their objective, read our [Cupid and lovers guide](/en/blog/werewolf-cupid-lovers/), including an example of defending a partner during discussion.

The Little Girl commonly gathers information by secretly watching during the wolves’ phase, at a risk. Follow your edition’s exact instructions for this unusual role rather than improvising.

Our [Little Girl guide](/en/blog/werewolf-little-girl-role/) explains how to separate a clear observation from a suspicion, with an example of the discussion after night.

## Which roles should beginners use?

Start with the core teams and a small number of special roles. Add complexity after players understand a full night-and-day cycle. Avoid combining several unfamiliar powers in a first round: it becomes harder to tell whether an unexpected result is a clue or a misunderstood rule.

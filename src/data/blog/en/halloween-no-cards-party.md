Your friends have arrived, the sweets are out, and nobody brought a game. **A phone with Werewolf Undercover lets you start a Halloween game without cards.** The app narrates while you play together. An external speaker can help everyone hear the voice, but it is optional if your phone is loud enough for the room.

Here is how to turn a corner of the living room into a village full of suspicious neighbours, from setting up to finishing your first game. For other ways to replace a deck, see our [guide to Werewolf without cards](/en/blog/werewolf-game-without-cards/).

## Before guests arrive: get the phone ready

Install the app before everyone gathers around the table. Check the official [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6740009341) or [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=xyz.bubbleappstudio.werewolfundercover) listing for device compatibility and available options.

Then prepare:

- A charged phone, with the app opened once to check that it starts.
- Seats arranged so everyone can see each other during discussions.
- A clear spot to put the phone between interactions.
- An optional speaker, already connected to the phone and ready for a sound check.

There is no need to buy equipment for the occasion. Listen to the phone from the furthest seat first. Moving chairs closer and turning down the music may be all you need.

## Make the narration clear without drowning out the players

Try the app’s narration before assigning the roles for the real game. If you use a speaker as your phone’s audio output, check that **the app’s voice** actually comes through it. Hearing your playlist through the speaker does not replace this check.

Put the speaker where everyone can hear it without obstructing the phone as it is passed around. Choose a volume that makes instructions clear without making people shout. If the connection or audio is unreliable, switch back to the phone speaker and bring the circle closer together.

Turn background music down or off during instructions. Save the songs for arrivals and breaks: nobody should miss a turn because the chorus covers the voice. Our [narrator app guide](/en/blog/werewolf-narrator-app/) explains the general phone setup.

## Set a Halloween mood that still lets people play

A dim lamp, a few decorations and your guests’ costumes can do plenty. Keep enough light to read the screen and see faces during discussions. A mask that muffles someone’s voice can wait for the group photo.

You could welcome the players with this opening:

> First the sweets vanished. Then the neighbours. Tonight, this village will have to think carefully about whom it trusts.

One person can read this opening before starting the game. Then follow the app’s instructions.

## Start the first game with beginners in mind

Wait until everyone who wants to play is seated. Friends who arrive later can join the next game, without interrupting a night phase to learn the rules.

1. **Introduce the teams.** Werewolves are hiding among the villagers. Explain the objectives and winning conditions for the game you have chosen.
2. **Explain the rhythm.** Night calls for quiet and secret actions. Day brings discussion and a vote. Our [Werewolf rules guide](/en/blog/how-to-play-werewolf/) helps explain that pattern.
3. **Cover only the roles in this game.** Choose a straightforward setup from the options available in your version for the first round.
4. **Follow the app’s instructions.** Give each person time to see their role and carry out the requested actions without anyone looking over their shoulder.

The group can hear the narration; secret information on the screen stays private. Avoid mirroring the screen to a television or leaving a role visible to neighbouring players.

For the first discussion, invite each person to name a suspicion and give a reason. “You changed your vote without explaining why” gives the group more to discuss than an accusation based on somebody’s costume.

## After the game: make one adjustment and play again

Take a short break to ask whether everyone could hear the instructions and understand their role. Fix whatever caused confusion, welcome any late arrivals and assign fresh roles if the group wants another game. You do not need to introduce every special character at once.

**Is an external speaker essential?** No. If everyone can hear the phone, it is enough for the narration. A speaker is an optional addition to try with your own setup.

**What about headphones?** They do not work for sharing narration with a whole room. Use audio the players can hear together.

**To keep track of a Werewolf game, separate your player list from your record of events.** Our [printable moderator sheet PDF](/downloads/werewolf-moderator-sheet-en.pdf) fits on one A4 page: current roles at the top, choices and outcomes below. It is for a human moderator and should stay hidden from the players.

Looking for words to read aloud? Open our [Werewolf moderator script](/en/blog/werewolf-moderator-script/) as well. This PDF is a blank tracking sheet; the script and its house rules are explained in that separate guide.

## Set up the sheet before dealing roles

Print the PDF in portrait orientation and fill it in with a pencil. There are twelve player rows and four round rows. Those are writing spaces, not a recommended setup or a player limit. Print another sheet if you need more room.

Number seats around the table and write down names. If two players share a name, add an initial or use the seat number to avoid recording a choice against the wrong person. Fill in roles only when your rules allow you to know them, and update the “Current role” column if a power changes an identity.

At the top, record the edition or variant you are using. Before starting, check how it handles tied votes, victory and revealing eliminated players’ roles. The sheet does not decide any of those rules for you.

## Separate a choice from its outcome

Give each event a label, such as “night 2” or “day 2 vote”. Record the choice in one column and the resolved outcome in the other. Use another sheet when several actions need separate rows.

For example, the Werewolves choose Alex at night. You write “target: Alex”. If a protection allowed by your rules prevents the elimination, the outcome differs from the original target. Crossing Alex off immediately would confuse an intended action with an actual elimination.

Keep your [night-call order](/en/blog/werewolf-night-role-order/) beside the sheet. A list of names helps you remember what happened; it does not replace resolving each power in the right sequence.

## Count votes without showing your notes

Keep the sheet facing you while checking who is still playing. Run the vote using your agreed procedure, count the choices, then record the outcome and any elimination. If you need to count again, use the visible votes or your edition’s procedure, rather than your memory of who “looked suspicious”.

A sheet in the middle of the table can reveal teams even if you never read it aloud. Keep your movements neutral too. Suddenly turning a page towards a player after an accusation might be interpreted as a clue.

## Start the next game with a clean record

Before beginning another night, update the list and check whether the game has ended. After the game, move any hosting notes to a separate page and start with a blank tracker. An old identity left beside a name can cause a mistake after roles are dealt again.

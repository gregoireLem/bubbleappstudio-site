**The Wild Child starts as a Villager and secretly chooses a role model on the first night. If that player is eliminated, the Wild Child becomes a Werewolf and joins the pack the following night, regardless of the model’s team.** This guide uses the [Pact rulebook, page 14, in French](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-asmodeeca/uploads/2023/07/WerewolvesThePact_FR_Rules.pdf#page=14). For another edition or the app, follow that game’s instructions.

## Who should you choose as your role model?

Think about how that person might contribute to the discussion. Someone who makes lots of accusations could become a voting target; someone who helps the village make progress could draw the wolves’ attention. A quiet player is not automatically safe either. These are possibilities, not reliable first-night predictions.

When trying the role for the first time, choose someone whose comments and votes you can keep track of. That gives you a decision to reflect on afterwards: “I thought Sam would last a while because he asked questions without taking over the discussion.” You have something to watch, beyond simply picking a friend.

Try not to take every accusation against your model personally. Ask what prompted it. You can defend someone’s reasoning without claiming they must be innocent. Our [Werewolf roles guide](/en/blog/werewolf-roles-explained/) explains how different characters have access to different information.

## An example: the vote against Sam

Imagine Lea is the Wild Child and chooses Sam. During the first discussion, she notices Noah switch his voting target without explaining why. She asks him about it. Later, the village votes Sam out; Lea is still playing and the game continues.

To handle this transition, Lea needs to remember **her new objective and what she has already said**. Her question about Noah is still part of the public discussion. If she suddenly defends him without giving a reason, other players may notice the reversal. She could instead pick up where she left off: “Noah, I still wanted to understand your first vote.”

Changing teams does not rewrite the earlier conversation. Our [guide to playing a Werewolf](/en/blog/werewolf-wolf-strategy/) offers examples of joining the debate while keeping your story consistent.

## What should the moderator write down?

Keep three details on your private sheet: **the Wild Child’s identity**, **the chosen model** and **the role’s current state**. When someone is eliminated, check any links involving that player before preparing the next phase. Do not rely solely on your memory of the initial deal.

Plan a discreet way to give any necessary instructions, too. Naming the model aloud or announcing that a role has changed would hand the village an extra clue. Our [night role order guide](/en/blog/werewolf-night-role-order/) helps you prepare the calls without improvising in front of the group.

## Playing with the Werewolf app

The Wild Child appears in the app’s version history on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6740009341). To try it on iPhone, check the roles offered in your installed version and read its instructions. The app provides narration for a group gathered around a phone; the Pact rule above does not replace the app’s directions.

On Android, check the [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=xyz.bubbleappstudio.werewolfundercover) and the roles available in your version. Before the first night, use our [narrator app guide](/en/blog/werewolf-narrator-app/) to prepare the sound and keep private information on the screen out of sight.

**Cupid chooses two lovers; becoming a lover adds a bond to a player’s role.** The crucial consequence is that one elimination can cause two. Depending on the couple’s teams, their winning objective may change too. Understand both your character and your bond before voting.

The rules below use **Le Pacte, an edition of The Werewolves of Miller’s Hollow**, as their reference. Follow the relevant instructions for other editions or a game in Werewolf Undercover. Our [roles guide](/en/blog/werewolf-roles-explained/) introduces the other characters.

## What does Cupid do on the first night?

In the [official Le Pacte rulebook, page 9, in French](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-asmodeeca/uploads/2023/07/WerewolvesThePact_FR_Rules.pdf), Cupid selects two players at the start and may include themselves. This is not a fresh choice each night. If one lover is eliminated, the other dies immediately; neither may harm the other, even as a bluff.

The moderator should explain the bond to everyone before the choice. Before dealing roles, ask a simple check question: “If your partner is eliminated, what happens to you?” Players should not discover the consequence during a decisive vote.

## Who wins together?

[Asmodee’s introduction to Cupid, in French](https://www.asmodee.fr/les-roles-des-loups-garous-de-thiercelieux-que-vous-allez-adorer/) describes the **Villager–Werewolf couple**: to win together, they must eliminate every other player. Their bond is more than an agreement to back each other during discussion.

Keep three pieces of information separate:

- **Your role:** the actions you can take.
- **Your partner:** the person whose survival is linked to yours.
- **Your objective:** the winning condition that applies to the couple in your version.

Recognising your partner does not by itself authorise you to inspect their card or secret screen. Check what your rules let you learn and communicate. If a power changes someone’s team during play, check how that affects victory too.

## How should Cupid choose the lovers?

Cupid does not automatically know everyone’s identity. Do not base your choice on the assumption that you already know who is a Werewolf. Think instead about the experience you want to play.

Choosing yourself puts you directly inside the bond and its shared risk. Choosing two other players lets you observe their behaviour afterwards. Neither choice guarantees success: a quiet player can become a target, and a confident speaker can misread a vote.

Keep the choice within the fiction of the game. There is no need to match people based on their real relationships or ask them to act out a romantic scene.

## An example of a useful defence

Imagine Mia and Noah are lovers. For this example, Mia is a Villager and Noah is a Werewolf. That explanatory viewpoint does not mean the players have been shown every identity.

During discussion, someone accuses Noah solely because he hesitated before voting. Mia can ask, “Which of Noah’s votes seems inconsistent to you?” That brings the discussion back to a specific point. Saying “Noah cannot be a Werewolf” is a different claim, and their bond does not prove it.

The couple needs to consider their shared survival. Sacrificing a partner to appear independent is not a valid tactic under the rule described above. Our [Villager guide](/en/blog/werewolf-villager-role-strategy/) explains how to argue without treating suspicions as facts.

## What should the moderator prepare?

The [Le Pacte summary on page 24](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-asmodeeca/uploads/2023/07/WerewolvesThePact_FR_Rules.pdf) lists Cupid’s call and the lovers’ recognition separately on the first night. Record the pair privately and prepare that sequence before starting. Our [night order guide](/en/blog/werewolf-night-role-order/) helps separate one-off calls from recurring actions.

When someone is eliminated, check the bond before announcing that play continues or a team has won. If a lover has an ability triggered by death, such as the [Hunter](/en/blog/werewolf-hunter-role/), check that interaction in your edition. Do not invent an exception after the target is known.

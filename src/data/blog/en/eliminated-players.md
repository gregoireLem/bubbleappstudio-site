**Once you’re eliminated in Werewolf, you can quietly follow the discussion or take a break until the next game.** Agree on both options before dealing roles. Nobody needs to sit there feeling left out, and the remaining players should be able to work things out without help from the sidelines.

These are hosting suggestions for an in-person evening. They do not add character powers or describe extra app features.

## Finish the elimination before becoming a spectator

Let the moderator or app resolve everything required by your rules first. Some characters have an action triggered by elimination. Once that is settled, do not return to discussion or voting unless your version expressly allows it.

Under [Andrew Plotkin’s rules](https://eblong.com/zarf/werewolf.html), eliminated players must remain silent, even when someone gets the history of the game wrong. Whether cards are revealed also depends on the version. Our [guide to revealing roles in Werewolf](/en/blog/can-you-reveal-your-role-in-werewolf/) explains those differences.

## Three ways to wait without giving anyone hints

### 1. Follow the reasoning, not just the hidden identities

Choose something to watch: “Which argument will change the next vote?” or “Will they return to the accusation from day one?” You can write down a prediction privately and compare it with what happened once the game is over.

For example, Leah thinks Sam’s vote will persuade the group. She writes that down without showing anyone. At the end, she can explain what convinced her, even if the village followed a different lead. The interest is in how the discussion unfolds. If you already know a character’s identity, guessing that identity is no longer the same challenge.

### 2. Save one question for the final reveal

Keep a specific question in mind: “Why did you change suspects on day two?” Afterwards, the player can explain their decision. Wait until the game ends to ask: even a question from the sidelines can steer the discussion.

### 3. Take an actual break

You can also quietly leave the circle and return for the next deal. Before starting, agree on somewhere to wait and how everyone will be called back. Keep conversations about roles out of earshot and noisy activities away from the game.

## The trouble with “I’m not saying anything, but…”

A lingering look, a thumbs-up or a laugh during an accusation can give a hint. Keep your notes to yourself and do not message someone who is still playing. Spectators should not become another team of advisers.

If a player looks to you for confirmation, stick to the agreement that eliminated players do not comment on anyone’s choices. That includes moments when you think the village has got everything wrong.

## Bring everyone back between games

After the final reveal, invite people who went out early to share a moment they enjoyed, if they want to. Then check who wants another game before dealing again. Do not automatically make the first eliminated person moderate the next one.

If the wait dragged, discuss [how much time to allow for the next game](/en/blog/how-long-does-werewolf-take/) and its debates. Our [first-time hosting guide](/en/blog/host-werewolf-first-time-players/) helps you explain these arrangements when welcoming a new group.

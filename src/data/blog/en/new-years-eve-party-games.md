**In Werewolf Undercover, the Innkeeper wakes when called and sets a glass in front of one player of their choice.** The Innkeeper does this at night. The chosen player discovers the glass when the village wakes, which is when the role calls for them to drink. For an adults’ New Year’s Eve game, agree before you start that water, raising an empty cup or passing are all fine.

## What happens on the Innkeeper’s turn?

Say you draw the Innkeeper. Jo is on your left and Sam is sitting opposite you. When the narrator calls your role, you quietly set the cup in front of Sam. Sam keeps his eyes closed. You settle back into your seat and wait for the narrator to wake the village. Sam sees the cup, raises it in a toast and puts it down. Play continues.

“Someone must know something about Sam,” Jo says during the discussion. What has she actually seen? The Innkeeper picked Sam. You might simply have picked the host for a New Year’s toast. The cup alone tells the village nothing about Sam’s team. If your group is learning several characters at once, use the [role guide](/en/blog/werewolf-roles-explained/) to explain what the Seer or Witch can do on their own turns.

## Set the table before dealing roles

Put an empty cup within reach in the middle of the table. Use it as a marker, so the chosen player can toast with their own drink if they like. That saves the Innkeeper from having to whisper “Whose glass has apple juice in it?” or reach across the phone with a full glass in the dark.

Before guests arrive, check whether the Innkeeper appears in the [role selection](/en/blog/werewolf-group-role-setup/) on the phone you’ll use. You can play Werewolf without this character. If you include the role, explain the cup to everyone before anyone sees their secret identity.

## Leave room for the countdown

With the party music on, sit in the chair furthest from the phone and check that you can hear the narrator. Our [narrator setup guide](/en/blog/werewolf-narrator-app/) covers where to put the phone so everyone can hear without seeing private choices. Friends who arrive halfway through can join the next game.

If it’s 11:50 and everyone wants to count down together, save the deal until after midnight. That way, nobody jumps up and opens their eyes just as the Innkeeper is choosing a player.

Ready for Werewolf, but nobody wants to spend the evening calling out the night phases? A narrator app can guide the game while your friends play around a phone. You still need to arrange the room, explain the rules you are using, and give everyone space to join the discussion.

Werewolf Undercover supports this in-person format: your friends gather in the same room and the app provides narration. Here is how to prepare a smooth game night, especially when some players are new.

Before setting up the phone, use our [official Werewolf download links for Android and iPhone](/en/blog/download-werewolf-android-iphone/) and check the installation advice.

## What does a Werewolf narrator app do?

In a traditional game, a moderator calls the characters during the night and guides the transition to daytime. An app provides the narration instructions built into its version of the game. Follow its guidance for assigning roles and taking actions instead of combining it with rules from a different edition.

The narrator does not choose your accusations or play for you. Discussion, alliances, and voting still belong to the group. Those decisions are what make one game different from the next.

If the game is new to you, start with our [beginner’s Werewolf rules](/en/blog/how-to-play-werewolf/). To replace the physical deck as well, read about [playing Werewolf without cards](/en/blog/werewolf-game-without-cards/).

## Prepare the phone before assigning roles

Check these details before the first night:

- **Everyone can hear the narration.** Test the volume from the most distant seat and turn down background music.
- **The phone has enough charge.** If it is plugged in, keep the cable out of the way when passing the device.
- **Notifications are silenced.** A private message or incoming call can interrupt an instruction.
- **Secret information stays private.** Do not leave the screen facing the group when it displays a role or confidential action.
- **Players have time to act.** Let each person read an instruction before moving on.

Choose someone to help operate the phone if needed. That does not give them permission to look at other players’ roles or interpret their decisions.

## Explain what matters for the first round

For new players, introduce the teams, voting, and only the powers included in your setup. Explaining every possible character at once can make the basic rules harder to remember.

Before assigning roles, ask: “Does everyone know when to close their eyes, when to speak, and how to vote?” Once roles are assigned, avoid questions that would force someone to reveal their character. ## Make room for everyone in the discussion

The narrator organizes the phases; the group still needs to share speaking time. Try an opening round where each person can name a suspicion and give a reason, then open the discussion. Nobody needs to make a speech. A single sentence is enough to participate.

Keep in-game accusations separate from personal remarks. “Your vote surprised me” gives the group something to discuss. Criticizing someone’s personality does not help find the werewolves.

When a player is eliminated, remind them of the rule your game uses. If eliminated players must stop sharing information, they can watch the remaining rounds without giving hints.

## Phone narration or online multiplayer?

A narrator app for friends in the same room and an online game with remote players solve different problems. This guide describes Werewolf Undercover as a game to share with the people around you. Using a phone does not mean everyone joins from a separate home.

Before game night, check your device’s store listing for compatibility and in-app purchase details. A free download does not necessarily include every option.

## What if someone misses an instruction?

Avoid asking aloud who just used a power. Check the instructions available in the app without exposing secret information. If the group can no longer continue fairly, starting a new game with newly assigned roles is often better than guessing.

After the game, identify what caused the confusion: volume, a role explanation, or speaking time. Fixing one practical issue can make the next round much easier to follow.

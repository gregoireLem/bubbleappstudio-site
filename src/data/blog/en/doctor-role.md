**The Doctor chooses a player to protect at night.** In [Stellar Factory’s Werewolf](https://playwerewolf.co/pages/rules), that player survives if the wolves picked the same target; the Doctor may choose themselves.

That idea may be familiar to your Mafia group. Before dealing roles, settle one important detail: **what limits does your version put on protection?** Our [Mafia vs Werewolf comparison](/en/blog/mafia-vs-werewolf/) helps you introduce the change of game.

## Can the Doctor save themselves?

Yes in the example above, but do not turn that option into a universal rule. The [Doctor card from Mafia Card Game](https://mafiacardgame.com/mafia-roles/doctor) prohibits choosing yourself on consecutive nights.

Before the first night, clarify these points for everyone:

| Question | Why the answer matters |
| --- | --- |
| Can you choose yourself? | A legal target in one game may be forbidden in another. |
| How often, and with what gap? | “Once per game” and “not on consecutive nights” are different restrictions. |
| Can you protect another player again? | A restriction on self-healing does not automatically settle repeat choices of other players. |
| Which effects does protection stop? | Do not read “save” as immunity to every power or the daytime vote. |

Keep the answers with your chosen rules. Asking a general question before roles are dealt avoids revealing your character to resolve a rules problem during the night.

## Example: plan your second choice

Use the restriction on the Mafia card linked above. Nora is the Doctor and chooses herself on night one. On night two, she needs another target: worrying about an attack does not create an exception.

During discussion, she can already consider two players she would like to keep in the game. Sam asked Lee to explain a changed vote; Jess promised to compare two conflicting accounts. Nora can choose based on what would help the next discussion, without being certain of either player’s team.

This separates **whether a choice is legal** from **whether it is useful**. A tempting decision does not become legal because it might succeed. And an eligible target is not necessarily the person the opposing team plans to attack.

## Choosing someone does not tell you their team

Your private note might say “night 2: Sam.” That should not become “Sam is innocent” merely because you picked him. Protection is an action; certainty about a player’s identity needs information that supports it.

In the morning, return to public evidence: what Sam said, how he voted and the answers he received. If he is still playing, keep examining his reasoning instead of automatically trusting him. If you decide to claim your role, distinguish your choice from anything the moderator actually told you.

## Doctor, Witch and Guard: check more than the name

A group used to the Doctor may encounter other protective characters. Read our [Witch potion guide](/en/blog/werewolf-witch-role-potions/) and the guide to [the Guard in our app](/en/blog/werewolf-guard-role/) separately. Check the character you are using instead of transferring an old healing rule to a new name.

For a game with a human moderator, prepare the [night calls](/en/blog/werewolf-night-role-order/) too. Keep a private record of the night number and target; review the restrictions before confirming a choice. Our [regular playing-card setup](/en/blog/play-mafia-werewolf-with-playing-cards/) explains assigning identities, but a card-to-role key does not replace the ability’s rules.

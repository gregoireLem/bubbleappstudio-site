To play the Witch well in Werewolf, separate two questions: **who would you rather keep in the game, and who do you have enough evidence to act against?** Answering one does not automatically answer the other. Focus on what your choice will mean for the next discussion and vote.

This guide is about decisions in a face-to-face game. Exact rules depend on your edition or app. Check them before roles are dealt so that a rules question during the night does not reveal your character.

## What does the Witch do in Werewolf?

In the version [described by Asmodee](https://www.asmodee.fr/les-roles-des-loups-garous-de-thiercelieux-que-vous-allez-adorer/), the Witch has two single-use potions: save the wolves’ victim or eliminate someone. Self-healing and using both in one night are allowed.

That last rule varies: [Wolfy allows only one potion per night](https://help.wolfy.net/fr/article/jouer-la-sorciere-1vt9r64/). Do not carry a rule from one game into another without checking. For Werewolf Undercover, read the instructions shown for your game; the examples below do not specify its settings.

## Three checks before you act

- **What you have left.** Remember your previous actions. Do not resolve uncertainty about an already-used resource by asking the whole group aloud.
- **What you actually know.** Information from the moderator, another player’s claim and your own hunch are different kinds of evidence.
- **What happens if you are wrong.** Picture the next discussion with one fewer person. Who will still be there to explain a vote, challenge an accusation or share information?

If a rule is unclear, ask the moderator using the method agreed for your game. Making up an exception halfway through the night can make later decisions inconsistent.

## Healing: think about the next day

Consider what the threatened player contributes to the discussion. Have they pointed out a specific contradiction that the group has not examined yet? Would their departure leave one person’s account unchallenged? Those questions are more useful than asking whether the player is your friend.

Saving someone does not commit you to supporting all their accusations afterwards. You have kept a person in the discussion; you have not endorsed their reasoning. Keep asking them for explanations, just as you would anyone else.

Waiting can preserve an option, but it also means accepting this night’s immediate consequences. There is no automatically correct choice every round. Compare the actual situation rather than simply trying to keep your resources until the end.

## Poison: put your reason into words first

Try finishing this sentence in your head: “I am targeting this player because…” If the answer is only “they are quiet,” “they accused me” or “everyone thinks they are acting strangely,” you probably need more information.

A more useful explanation might be: “Noah says he voted against Alex before Alex accused him, but two players described the events in the opposite order.” That is still something to investigate, not absolute proof. You could prepare a question for the daytime discussion instead of deciding immediately.

Do not confuse a repeated accusation with several independent clues. Three people repeating the same suspicion do not necessarily give you three more reasons to act.

## Example: the cost of choosing the wrong target

Imagine six players remain: four village players, including the Witch, and two werewolves. The wolves target another village player. No other powers interfere, and assume the wolves win as soon as they reach equal numbers. The reader knows these teams; the Witch may not.

- **If no potion is used**, the day would begin with three village players and two wolves.
- **If only healing is used**, all six players would remain.
- **If poison hits another village player and healing is not used**, two village players and two wolves would remain. Under the assumed win condition, the wolves would win.

This example does not tell you whom to target. It shows why an extra elimination can matter more than a missed opportunity. Before acting, count the remaining players and separate confirmed teams from assumptions.

## What should you say during the discussion?

You can contribute without immediately naming your role. Revisit votes, ask for the order of events and separate facts from interpretations. If your group allows role claims and you choose to speak, describe your action and its limits accurately. Do not present a deduction as something you know for certain.

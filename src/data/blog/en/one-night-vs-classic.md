**Classic Werewolf** moves through repeated nights and days, with players eliminated along the way. **One Night Ultimate Werewolf** ends after a single night, a discussion, and a final vote. That difference changes both the evidence you discuss and how you prepare your group.

Looking for an app to go with your One Night box? Start with [Bézier Games’ official product page](https://beziergames.com/collections/all-games/products/one-night-ultimate-werewolf). Your narration needs to match the edition and characters you are playing.

## Classic Werewolf: follow the story over several rounds

In the format described by [Stellar Factory’s Werewolf rules](https://playwerewolf.co/pages/rules), the Werewolves act at night, and the village discusses and votes during the day. Eliminated players leave the game; the cycle continues until a team wins. Our [guide to playing Werewolf](/en/blog/how-to-play-werewolf/) explains this overall structure.

You can build a case from the history of the game: who defended someone yesterday, changed their mind, or voted together? Suppose Alex accuses Ben after defending him the previous round. Ask what happened to change Alex’s view. That shift is not proof of a lie, but it gives you a specific question to discuss.

Choose this format if your group enjoys watching alliances develop. Explain what eliminated players can do before starting, so they do not accidentally keep sharing information.

## One Night: reconstruct what happened overnight

[Bézier Games describes One Night Ultimate Werewolf](https://beziergames.com/collections/all-games/products/one-night-ultimate-werewolf) as a game without early player elimination or a dedicated moderator. Everyone stays involved until the final vote.

Another important difference is that some characters move cards. The publisher’s [Getting Started Guide](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0740/4855/files/ONUW_Getting_Started_for_BGG.pdf?v=1687188092) explains that your final role depends on the card in front of you, which may have changed during the night. Werewolves do not select a nighttime victim.

A useful question becomes: “Are you describing the card you saw at the start, or do you know it stayed in front of you?” An honest statement about a starting role may not establish a player’s final team. This does not give you permission to peek at your card after the night.

For your first game, explain only the characters you have selected. A single night can still involve powers that need a careful introduction.

## Settle three things before closing your eyes

- **Name the version.** “Let’s play Werewolf” does not yet tell everyone which game you mean.
- **Explain voting and victory.** Take the win conditions and tie rules from the same rulebook instead of combining variants.
- **Do a practice explanation without secrets.** Ask someone to repeat when players can talk and when they vote before you hand out roles.

Let the group’s preference guide your choice: an investigation that develops across several days, or one night to untangle together before a decisive vote.

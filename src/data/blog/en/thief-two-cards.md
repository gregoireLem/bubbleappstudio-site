**In the Thief rules described in Le Pacte, you inspect two undealt cards on the first night and may take one in exchange for your role. If both are Werewolves, you must swap.** This guide concerns that version of The Werewolves of Miller’s Hollow, rather than every Thief or Robber character in other games.

## Why are there two cards in the middle?

The [official French rulebook, page 13](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-asmodeeca/uploads/2023/07/WerewolvesThePact_FR_Rules.pdf#page=13) adds two ordinary Villager cards to the selected deck before shuffling and dealing. The two cards left over become the Thief’s secret options. They need not be the two Villagers you added: shuffling can leave a different character in the middle.

When planning your [group’s role setup](/en/blog/werewolf-group-role-setup/), count the people receiving roles, the moderator and the unused cards separately. Extra cards deliberately included for this role are not a dealing mistake.

## Choose from the middle, not another player’s hand

Your options are the cards in the middle. You do not secretly take the identity of the player beside you. Once you exchange cards, play your new character instead of looking for another swap each night. Use the rulebook to check when that character acts.

Before choosing, think about the kind of role you want to play. Would you enjoy bringing information into a discussion or trying to bluff alongside the pack? If a power is unfamiliar, take a moment to read it. Recognizing a character’s name is not the same as knowing when to act.

## An example: two players claim to be the Seer

Imagine a game with just one Seer card and no further role changes. The spare cards are Seer and Villager, and you take the Seer. During the first discussion, Sam announces, “I’m the Seer.”

You know that claim conflicts with your own card. Everyone else hears two competing stories. Saying “I started as the Thief” does not let them see what you saw. If you decide to speak, explain your sequence of roles clearly and avoid adding an unsupported conclusion: claiming a false role does not, on its own, prove that Sam is a Werewolf.

Remember the boundary of your knowledge. You know what you chose, not what every other player received. Being certain about one card does not turn all your suspicions into evidence.

## A first-night reminder for the moderator

Mark this opening choice clearly in your [night-call order](/en/blog/werewolf-night-role-order/). Leave room in your notes for the player’s final role: the person you initially recorded as the Thief may now be someone else. Confirm the choice discreetly before moving on, without a comment that gives the village a clue.

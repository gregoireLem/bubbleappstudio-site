**Yes, you can use a regular deck of playing cards to assign Mafia or Werewolf roles.** Each selected card stands for a character; you are not taking tricks or adding up card values. Agree on a role key, set aside the cards you will use and deal them face down.

The deck does not determine anyone’s powers. Choose your rules first. If your friends know Mafia and want to try Werewolf, our [Mafia vs Werewolf comparison](/en/blog/mafia-vs-werewolf/) explains what to check before swapping character names.

## A nine-card example for ten people

This example uses [Stellar Factory’s setup](https://playwerewolf.co/pages/rules): **one Moderator, two Werewolves, one Seer, one Doctor and five Villagers**. Nine people receive secret roles; the tenth moderates outside both teams. For a different headcount, see our [guide to setting up roles for your group](/en/blog/werewolf-group-role-setup/).

Here is our suggested card-to-role key using a standard 52-card deck. It is a convenient convention, not an official code shared by every Mafia or Werewolf version.

| Cards to keep | Assigned role | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ace of spades and ace of clubs | Werewolves | 2 |
| King of hearts | Seer | 1 |
| Queen of hearts | Doctor | 1 |
| 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 of hearts | Ordinary Villagers | 5 |

**That makes nine cards.** Set aside the other 43 cards from the 52-card deck, plus any jokers, before dealing. The moderator does not need a tenth role card.

## Explain the key before shuffling

Lay out the nine cards face up and explain the mapping. Use full card names: “king of hearts,” not just “king.” In this example, the king of spades represents nobody because it is not in the selected deck.

Next, explain the powers from your chosen rules. A card’s rank does not explain its ability: a queen does not automatically act as a Witch just because another group uses that convention. Leave the card-to-role key where everyone can read it, but keep any list linking players’ names to secret roles private.

Check that the card backs match and that a crease or mark does not identify a particular card. Avoid filling a gap with a card from a different deck whose back stands out.

## Deal one role to each player

1. **Count again:** nine cards and nine recipients.
2. **Shuffle face down**, then deal one card to each player. Do not keep the order from the table above.
3. **Let everyone check their own card privately.** They then place it face down in front of them.
4. **Start your version’s night calls.** The moderator prepares private notes and uses the same ruleset for the calls and the powers.

Our [night order guide](/en/blog/werewolf-night-role-order/) helps with that preparation. Do not show a neighbor your card to ask what it means. Check the public role key, or use the discreet way of asking the moderator that you agreed on before play.

## What if the selected deck is wrong?

Imagine counting only eight cards before dealing: the six of hearts is still in the box. Add it, recount and shuffle the whole selection.

If players have already looked at their cards when you discover a missing or duplicate role, **collect every card and deal the corrected selection again**. Publicly handing someone the only missing role could reveal their team. This is our practical suggestion for fixing a setup mistake, not a character ability.

Before a rematch, collect cards from eliminated players too. Rebuild the original nine-card selection before shuffling; nobody carries their previous identity into the new game.

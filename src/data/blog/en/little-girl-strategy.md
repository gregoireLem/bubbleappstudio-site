**The Little Girl can spy on the wolves while they are awake, at the risk of being discovered.** The next challenge is using what you saw wisely. Catching a glimpse of a shape is not the same as clearly identifying a player.

This guide offers advice for an in-person game. The exact power and the procedure when someone spots you depend on your version. Check both before assigning roles.

## When may the Little Girl look?

The [official French Le Pacte rulebook, page 10](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-asmodeeca/uploads/2023/07/WerewolvesThePact_FR_Rules.pdf), permits discreet watching only while the wolves are awake. If spotted, she may replace their victim. She cannot open her eyes fully to pretend to be a wolf.

Those details belong to that edition. With an app, read its role description and follow its instructions. A shared character name does not transfer every rule from one game to another.

For planning the different phases, see our guide to [Werewolf night role order](/en/blog/werewolf-night-role-order/).

## Have a specific question in mind

Before taking a risk, ask what you hope to check. “Did I recognize that person correctly?” is a more useful question than trying to see the whole table at any cost.

Do not assume a longer look will necessarily give you better information. With a poor viewing angle, you may collect more impressions without identifying anyone more clearly. Acknowledge that limit rather than filling in what you could not see.

Remember three things: **the phase, the observation and your confidence in it**. For example: “During the wolves’ action, I recognized Alex; I am not sure about the person beside them.” That summary helps you separate evidence from inference when morning comes.

## An example: two suspicions, one clear observation

Imagine you clearly see Alex taking part in the wolves’ choice. You also notice movement near Sam, without recognizing who is moving.

During the discussion, Alex defends Sam. That defense is worth asking about, but it does not turn an uncertain glimpse into a second identification. You could ask, “Why have you ruled Sam out?” Listen to the answer before drawing another conclusion.

If you decide to explain what you saw, and your table permits role claims, a fair account would be: “I clearly recognized Alex during the wolves’ action. I did not identify the other person.” Avoid “I saw Alex and Sam”: the second half goes beyond your information.

This example assumes no power that disguises an observed identity. If your setup includes such effects, you also need to account for them.

## When should you share what you know?

Consider what your information could change about the current vote. If the group is about to act on an error you can correct, speaking up may help. If you only have an ambiguous glimpse, begin with questions and be clear about your uncertainty.

Claiming a role does not guarantee that the village will believe you. Prepare a short explanation, then let others discuss it. Do not show a secret card or screen to prove your account unless the rules allow it.

If half the family is still clearing plates, try **What’s missing?** People can join between trips to the kitchen. If there’s barely room for a coffee cup, play **Three clues** out loud. Save **Werewolf** for the point when everyone who fancies a bluffing game can sit down and stay for the vote.

## Over coffee: what’s missing from the tray?

Set five different objects on a tray or placemat. A satsuma, teaspoon, paper star, ribbon and cork will do. Put the tray where everyone can see it, name each item aloud and give people as long as they like to look.

Ask everyone to close their eyes. Hide one item under a napkin and move the others around a little. When the family opens their eyes, they get to **work out the answer together**. If the teaspoon is missing and they need a hint, try “You can still tie up a present, but what were you stirring your coffee with?” Bring the item back and invite someone else to hide the next one. You can stop after any reveal to make room for dessert.

## Still at the table: guess it in three clues

One person thinks of a familiar object and gives clues one at a time. After each clue, the table can confer and offer one guess. Here’s a first round to read aloud:

1. “I spend most of the year at the back of a drawer.”
2. “In December I come out with the rolling pin.”
3. “Press me into dough and you’ll get a star.”

It’s a **cookie cutter**. “That thing for cutting out biscuits” counts too. If the third clue leaves everyone stumped, give them the answer. Ask a pair to make up the next round together. That gives someone who’s happy to invent clues but shy about speaking a chance to play alongside someone who likes delivering them.

## Once dessert is cleared: bring out Werewolf

Picture a few cousins suggesting Werewolf while the last plates are being stacked. Before you count players, give everyone the premise. Each player gets a secret role. The wolves act at night, and by day the village discusses its suspicions and votes. Anyone eliminated sits out the debate. Someone who’d like to watch the first game can sit where they can see faces while private screens stay out of view.

Pull the chairs close enough that a quiet voice can carry. With **Werewolf Undercover**, a phone can narrate your in-person game. Turn down the Christmas music and listen to a bit of narration from the farthest seat. Count the people playing this round, then [pick a set of roles for your group](/en/blog/werewolf-group-role-setup/). Before anyone sees a role, give the village a name by a show of hands. “Are we Clementine or Gingerbread?” gets everyone practising the vote while the stakes are low. Then explain the [rules you’ll use](/en/blog/how-to-play-werewolf/). If the first discussion goes quiet, the [guide to hosting first-time players](/en/blog/host-werewolf-first-time-players/) has questions to get it started.

If someone comes through the door carrying the pudding just as you’re about to hand out roles, leave the roles for later. A three-clue riddle can travel down the table while dessert does.

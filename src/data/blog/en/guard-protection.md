**In the iPhone version of Werewolf Undercover, the Guard can protect one player from the wolves’ attack each night.** When choosing, think back to the last discussion. Who would the wolves want out of tomorrow’s conversation? You might trust one player most and still have a better reason to protect someone else.

## Use the last vote to pick a player

Suppose Maya noticed that Ben announced he would vote for Sam, then voted for Jo without explaining why. Maya plans to ask him about it tomorrow. Meanwhile, several players already think Alex is a wolf and intend to vote for him next.

**Maya is a reasonable pick for the Guard.** Keeping her in the game would let her follow up on that vote. The wolves also have a reason to leave Alex alone: while the village argues about him, it spends less time looking elsewhere. Either read could be wrong. To weigh the other side of that decision, see [how a wolf might choose a night target](/en/blog/werewolf-wolf-strategy/).

On the first night you may have almost nothing to go on. Choose someone the game allows you to protect. The next day, listen for one thing you can bring to your next decision, such as a vote someone changed or a question they promised to answer.

## What does a survivor tell the Guard?

You protected Maya. At dawn she is still sitting at the table. The wolves might have targeted her and hit your protection. They might also have gone after someone else. **Seeing her survive tells you neither whom the wolves chose nor which side Maya is on.**

That matters when you speak. “Ben, you said Sam yesterday. What made you switch to Jo?” gives everyone a public event they can discuss. “I know the wolves went after Maya” claims knowledge you haven’t gained from choosing her.

## Can the Guard protect themselves or pick the same player twice?

The [App Store notes for version 2.70](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6740009341) confirm protection against the wolves at night. Check the instructions for the game you’re playing for self-protection, repeat targets and interactions with other powers. Treat the village vote as a separate decision. Protection from the wolves’ attack gives you no basis for promising someone a free pass at the vote.

To try the role on iPhone, check which version is installed on the phone you’ll use. The app narrates an in-person game. Our [narrator setup guide](/en/blog/werewolf-narrator-app/) explains how to let everyone hear the voice while keeping private choices out of sight. Get the game on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6740009341) or check the [Android listing on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=xyz.bubbleappstudio.werewolfundercover) for your device.
